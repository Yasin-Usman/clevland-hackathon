**The Last Train**

---

Oren missed the last train on purpose.

He told himself he hadn't. He stood on the platform watching the lights shrink into the tunnel and thought, well, that's that, as though it had simply happened to him. But he had seen the doors closing and he had not moved, and some honest part of him knew the difference.

It was 11:47 on a Wednesday night and he had nowhere to be until morning.

The station emptied quickly. A janitor came through with a wide push-broom, moving in long slow arcs. Oren sat on a bench and watched him work. The janitor was an older man, unhurried, listening to something through a single earbud. He moved the way people move when they have done a thing so many times it no longer requires thought.

After a while the janitor looked up and noticed Oren.

"Missed it?" he asked.

"Yeah."

The janitor nodded, unsurprised, and kept sweeping. Then, after a moment: "First time?"

Oren considered the question more carefully than it probably warranted. "No," he said.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

He told himself he hadn't. He stood on the platform watching the lights shrink into the tunnel and thought, well, that's that, as though it had simply happened to him. But he had seen the doors closing and he had not moved, and some honest part of him knew the difference.

It was 11:47 on a Wednesday night and he had nowhere to be until morning.

The station emptied quickly. A janitor came through with a wide push-broom, moving in long slow arcs. Oren sat on a bench and watched him work. The janitor was an older man, unhurried, listening to something through a single earbud. He moved the way people move when they have done a thing so many times it no longer requires thought.

After a while the janitor looked up and noticed Oren.

"Missed it?" he asked.

"Yeah."

The janitor nodded, unsurprised, and kept sweeping. Then, after a moment: "First time?"

Oren considered the question more carefully than it probably warranted. "No," he said.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

He told himself he hadn't. He stood on the platform watching the lights shrink into the tunnel and thought, well, that's that, as though it had simply happened to him. But he had seen the doors closing and he had not moved, and some honest part of him knew the difference.

It was 11:47 on a Wednesday night and he had nowhere to be until morning.

The station emptied quickly. A janitor came through with a wide push-broom, moving in long slow arcs. Oren sat on a bench and watched him work. The janitor was an older man, unhurried, listening to something through a single earbud. He moved the way people move when they have done a thing so many times it no longer requires thought.

After a while the janitor looked up and noticed Oren.

"Missed it?" he asked.

"Yeah."

The janitor nodded, unsurprised, and kept sweeping. Then, after a moment: "First time?"

Oren considered the question more carefully than it probably warranted. "No," he said.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

The janitor smiled at the floor. "There's a diner on Clement Street. Open all night. Good pie."

He swept on. Eventually he disappeared through a door marked STAFF and Oren was alone in the station, which hummed faintly with the particular quiet of a place built for noise, now empty.

He sat there a while longer. Then he got up and went to find the diner.

The pie was, in fact, good.

He ate it slowly and watched the street outside the window and felt, for reasons he couldn't explain and didn't try to, that he had made the right choice — though he still wasn't ready to say what the choice had been, exactly, or what it meant, or what came next.

That was fine. He had all night.

**The Last Train**

---

Oren missed the last train on purpose.

He told himself he hadn't. He stood on the platform watching the lights shrink into the tunnel and thought, well, that's that, as though it had simply happened to him. But he had seen the doors closing and he had not moved, and some honest part of him knew the difference.

It was 11:47 on a Wednesday night and he had nowhere to be until morning.

The station emptied quickly. A janitor came through with a wide push-broom, moving in long slow arcs. Oren sat on a bench and watched him work. The janitor was an older man, unhurried, listening to something through a single earbud. He moved the way people move when they have done a thing so many times it no longer requires thought.

After a while the janitor looked up and noticed Oren.
